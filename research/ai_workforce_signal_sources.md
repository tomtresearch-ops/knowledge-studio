## 1. KEY INDIVIDUAL VOICES & RESEARCHERS

### A. AI Industry Leaders Making Workforce Predictions

**Sam Altman** — CEO, OpenAI
- Says AI "superintelligence" will eventually outperform human CEOs. Acknowledges some "AI washing" where companies blame AI for layoffs that would happen anyway, but confirms real displacement is coming. More moderate than Amodei on timeline — says no evidence yet of rapid widespread displacement. Warned older workers (62+) face the steepest adaptation curve.
- Why he matters for 22-30s: His predictions about which jobs get automated set the narrative. When Altman speaks, hiring managers listen.
- Feed: OpenAI blog (https://openai.com/blog), @sama on X

**Dario Amodei** — CEO, Anthropic
- Most aggressive predictions among major AI CEOs. Warned AI could spike unemployment to 10-20% within 1-5 years. Predicted half of entry-level white-collar jobs could be eliminated. Called the coming disruption "unusually painful." In direct disagreement with Altman and Huang on severity.
- Why he matters for 22-30s: His entry-level job predictions are directly about this audience. If even partially right, career planning changes fundamentally.
- Feed: Anthropic blog (https://www.anthropic.com/news), Dario's essays (https://darioamodei.com/)

**Daniela Amodei** — President, Anthropic
- Counterbalances brother Dario's doom framing. Said jobs AI can do "without help from people is vanishingly small." Believes "humans plus AI together create more meaningful work." Notably said humanities degrees will be "more important than ever" and that Anthropic prioritizes empathy/communication over coding in hiring.
- Why she matters for 22-30s: Her hiring philosophy at a leading AI company signals what skills actually get you hired in the AI era.
- Feed: Anthropic blog, interviews

**Jensen Huang** — CEO, NVIDIA
- Disagrees with Amodei on "almost everything." Says AI will "create a lot of jobs" — plumbers, electricians, construction workers, chip factory builders — with six-figure salaries. Sees infrastructure buildout as the near-term employment story, not displacement.
- Why he matters for 22-30s: Represents the "AI creates jobs" counter-narrative. Points to non-traditional career paths (trades, infrastructure) that 22-30s may overlook.
- Feed: NVIDIA blog (https://blogs.nvidia.com/), GTC keynotes

**Mustafa Suleyman** — CEO, Microsoft AI
- Microsoft's AI chief has said AI can replace "every white-collar job" in 18 months, with "human-level performance on most, if not all, professional tasks."
- Why he matters for 22-30s: When the person deploying AI into the world's most-used productivity suite says this, it affects every office job.
- Feed: Microsoft WorkLab (https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/)

### B. Academic Researchers Studying AI & Labor

**Ethan Mollick** — Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania
- Associate Professor; Co-Director, Generative AI Labs at Wharton. Named TIME's Most Influential People in AI. Author of NYT bestseller *Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI*. Writes every newsletter post himself. Research focus: practical implications of AI for work and education.
- Why he matters for 22-30s: Most accessible academic voice on AI + work. Translates research into actionable career guidance. His experiments showing AI's impact on real work tasks are essential reading.
- Newsletter: **One Useful Thing** — https://www.oneusefulthing.org/ (Substack)
- RSS: https://www.oneusefulthing.org/feed

**Erik Brynjolfsson** — Stanford University
- Jerry Yang & Akiko Yamazaki Professor; Director, Stanford Digital Economy Lab; Senior Fellow, Stanford HAI. Co-authored *The Second Machine Age* and *Machine, Platform, Crowd*. Lead author of "Generative AI at Work" — one of the first major studies observing real-world AI impact on workplace productivity at scale.
- Why he matters for 22-30s: His research provides actual data on how AI changes productivity and who benefits. Stanford Digital Economy Lab is the gold standard.
- Feed: Stanford Digital Economy Lab (https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/)
- Key resource: AI & Future of Work Resources page (https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/ai-future-of-work-resources/)

**Daron Acemoglu** — MIT (Nobel Laureate, 2024)
- Institute Professor at MIT. Won 2024 Nobel in Economics. Core thesis: "We have the wrong direction for AI — using it too much for automation and not enough for providing expertise and information to workers." Estimated AI will produce only a modest 1.1-1.6% GDP increase over next decade. Author of "The Simple Macroeconomics of AI" and "Tasks, Automation, and the Rise in U.S. Wage Inequality."
- Why he matters for 22-30s: The most credible skeptic of AI hype. His "augmentation vs. automation" framework helps young workers understand which jobs are threatened vs. enhanced.
- Feed: MIT Economics (https://economics.mit.edu/), his papers on NBER

**David Autor** — MIT
- Ford Professor of Economics. Co-directed MIT Task Force on the Work of the Future (2018-2020). Now co-directs MIT Shaping the Future of Work Initiative. Research on how technology creates polarization in labor markets — hollowing out middle-skill jobs while growing high and low-skill work.
- Why he matters for 22-30s: His "task framework" (not whole jobs, but specific tasks get automated) is the most useful mental model for career planning.
- Feed: MIT Shaping the Future of Work (https://shapingwork.mit.edu/)

**Carl Benedikt Frey** — University of Oxford
- Dieter Schwarz Associate Professor of AI & Work, Oxford Internet Institute. Director, Future of Work Programme at Oxford Martin School. Co-authored the landmark 2013 paper "The Future of Employment" (47% of jobs at risk of automation — 17,000+ citations). Revisited estimates in 2024 for the generative AI era. Latest book: *How Progress Ends* (2025, shortlisted for FT Business Book of the Year).
- Why he matters for 22-30s: His occupation-level risk estimates remain the most-cited framework. His updated 2024 analysis factors in generative AI specifically.
- Feed: Oxford Martin School (https://www.oxfordmartin.ox.ac.uk/)

**Joseph Fuller** — Harvard Business School
- Professor of Management Practice. Co-director, Managing the Future of Work project. Research on degree inflation, hidden workers, and how AI-powered Applicant Tracking Systems systematically screen out qualified candidates. Co-authored "Dismissed by Degrees" and "Hidden Workers, Untapped Talent."
- Why he matters for 22-30s: His research on degree inflation and ATS bias directly affects how 22-30s get (or don't get) hired. The skills-based hiring shift he documents is the most actionable trend for this audience.
- Podcast: HBS Managing the Future of Work (https://www.hbs.edu/managing-the-future-of-work/podcast/)

**Matt Sigelman** — President, Burning Glass Institute
- Career dedicated to unlocking mobility through skills. Burning Glass Institute collaborates with HBS Managing the Future of Work and the Schultz Family Foundation on the American Opportunity Index. Burning Glass data (via Lightcast) underpins much of the skills-based hiring research.
- Why he matters for 22-30s: His data shows which skills are actually in demand vs. what job postings claim. The 4 skills now required in 1-in-8 job postings (AI/ML, cloud computing, product management, social media) directly guide career investments.
- Feed: Burning Glass Institute (https://www.burningglassinstitute.org/news)

- Key URL: https://www.mckinsey.com/mgi/overview

**Boston Consulting Group (BCG)**
- **AI at Work** annual report (most recent: 2025). Finds mature AI adoption improves ROI 20-30%. Research on how organizations are actually deploying AI across functions.
- Key URL: https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/ai-at-work-momentum-builds-but-gaps-remain

**Deloitte**
- Predicts 1 in 4 companies using GenAI will launch agentic AI pilots by 2025, reaching 50% by 2027. Publishes annual Human Capital Trends report.

**PwC**
- **Global AI Jobs Barometer** (2025) — found 100% of industries now increasing AI usage, including traditionally slow adopters (mining, agriculture). Also published "Global Workforce Hopes & Fears Survey" with specific data on entry-level workers.
- Key URL: https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/issues/workforce/ai-entry-level-careers.html

**Gartner**
- Predicts AI will flatten 20% of organizations by 2026, eliminating 50%+ of middle management positions. Their predictions on management structure changes are some of the most cited in boardrooms.
- Key URL: https://www.gartner.com/en/topics/future-of-work

### B. Academic & Policy Research Institutions

**Stanford Digital Economy Lab** (Erik Brynjolfsson)
- Interdisciplinary research on how digital technologies transform work, organizations, economy. Partners with ADP Research Institute on employment data. Published "Canaries in the Coal Mine" on early-career AI impact.
- Key URL: https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/
- AI & Future of Work Resources: https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/ai-future-of-work-resources/

**Stanford HAI (Human-Centered AI)**
- Publishes the annual **AI Index Report** — the most comprehensive data compilation on AI progress, adoption, and economic impact globally.
- Key URL: https://hai.stanford.edu/

**MIT Shaping the Future of Work Initiative**
- Successor to the MIT Work of the Future Task Force (Autor/Mindell). Includes Automation Clinic and industry working group on Generative AI and Work. Core thesis: focus on augmenting workers, not replacing them.
- Key URL: https://shapingwork.mit.edu/

**NBER (National Bureau of Economic Research)**
- Publishes the definitive working papers on AI and labor economics. Key recent papers:
  - "Artificial Intelligence and the Labor Market" (Hampole et al., 2025) — task-level AI exposure measures 2010-2023
  - "Technology and Labor Markets: Past, Present, and Future" — AI shifting demand toward lower-education occupations
  - "The Labor Market Effects of Generative AI" — LLM adoption rose from 30.1% to 38.3% among US workers in 2025
- Key URL: https://www.nber.org/ (papers require subscription; working paper PDFs often freely available)

**Brookings Institution / Hamilton Project**
- Future of Work research program. Focuses on equity dimensions of AI automation (disproportionate impact on Black and Hispanic workers). Hosts events on "pro-worker AI." Collaborates with AEI and Harvard Kennedy School on the Workforce Futures Initiative.
- Key URL: https://www.brookings.edu/topics/future-of-work/

**Institute for the Future of Work (IFOW)** — UK
- Co-founded by Nobel laureate Sir Christopher Pissarides. Focuses on how technology affects job quality, not just job quantity. Published Pissarides Review on Future of Work and Wellbeing. Identified 3,500+ "core" automation-resilient skills.
- Key URL: https://www.ifow.org/
- Publications: https://www.ifow.org/resources/publications

**Budget Lab at Yale**
- Non-partisan policy research. Tracks AI's impact on labor market using CPS data. Finding as of late 2025: NO detectable employment impact from AI yet — occupational dissimilarity metrics remain flat and within historical ranges. Provides the skeptical empirical counterweight to hype.
- Key URL: https://budgetlab.yale.edu/topic/artificial-intelligence

**Georgetown CSET (Center for Security and Emerging Technology)**
- Research on AI workforce training, community college role in reskilling, and AI talent pipelines. Key finding: 80% of US workers may have 10%+ of tasks affected by LLMs.
- Key URL: https://cset.georgetown.edu/

**Burning Glass Institute**
- Skills-based labor market analytics. Collaborates with HBS and Schultz Family Foundation on the American Opportunity Index. Tracks which credentials actually lead to mobility. Data backbone via Lightcast.
- Key URL: https://www.burningglassinstitute.org/

## 4. DATA SOURCES — Actual Labor Market Signal

### A. Primary Job Market Data

**Indeed Hiring Lab**
- Data portal with Job Postings Index, Wage Tracker, and AI Tracker (share of postings mentioning AI). AI Tracker hit 4.2% in Dec 2025. Nearly 45% of data & analytics postings contain AI terms. Data freely available under Creative Commons 4.0.
- Data portal: https://data.indeed.com/
- Blog: https://www.hiringlab.org/
- GitHub (raw data): https://github.com/hiring-lab/
  - Job postings tracker: https://github.com/hiring-lab/job_postings_tracker
  - Wage tracker: https://github.com/hiring-lab/indeed-wage-tracker
- RSS: https://www.hiringlab.org/feed/

**LinkedIn Economic Graph**
- Workforce data covering AI skill adoption, job transitions, and labor market dynamics. Found AI has already added 1.3M new jobs. Fastest-growing AI skills: Custom GPTs, AI Productivity, AI Agents. Published "Work Change Report" and "Future of Work Report: AI at Work."
- Key URLs:
  - https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/
  - AI reports: https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/research/ai-skills-resources
  - Work Change Report: https://economicgraph.linkedin.com/research/work-change-report

**Glassdoor Economic Research**
- Annual **Worklife Trends** report. 2026 findings: "forever layoffs" (rolling small cuts replacing big rounds), career opportunity ratings dropping, 149% surge in "misalignment" mentions. Data from 7M+ salaries. Tracks employee sentiment toward AI.
- Key URL: https://www.glassdoor.com/blog/category/research/

**ADP Research Institute**
- Processes payroll data from 26M+ US employees. Monthly ADP National Employment Report (co-developed with Stanford Digital Economy Lab). Found substantial decline in early-career employment for AI-exposed occupations (software dev, customer support).
- Key URLs:
  - https://adpemploymentreport.com/
  - Research blog: https://www.adpresearch.com/data-lab/
  - Pay Insights: https://payinsights.adp.com/

**Lightcast** (formerly Burning Glass Technologies + Emsi)
- 1B+ job postings database. Provides the data backbone for Burning Glass Institute, government agencies, and academic researchers. Published Global AI Skills Outlook. Tracks real-time skill demand across industries.
- Key URL: https://lightcast.io/
- Research: https://lightcast.io/resources/research/the-lightcast-global-ai-skills-outlook

**Revelio Labs**
- Universal workforce database from hundreds of millions of public employment records. Tracks workforce flows, hiring patterns, and skills demand. Provides real-time alternative data on AI's labor impact.
- Key URL: https://www.reveliolabs.com/

**Upwork Research Institute**
- Monthly hiring report on freelance economy (1M+ US jobs/year on platform). Found 28% of US knowledge workers freelance ($1.5T earnings). Freelancers outpace employees in AI adoption (54% advanced proficiency vs. 38%). AI-related work up 60% YoY.
- Key URL: https://www.upwork.com/research
