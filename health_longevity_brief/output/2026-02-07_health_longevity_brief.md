**The mitochondrial-immune axis is consolidating as a master lever for inflammaging.** Three independent findings this week (TFAM-Tregs, mTOR-hepatic stress, NCLX-mediated Ca²⁺ release from mitochondria) all converge on mitochondrial function as upstream of both metabolic and immune dysfunction in aging. The implication: targeting mitochondrial biogenesis, dynamics (fission/fusion), or metabolic efficiency may have broader payoff than single-pathway interventions. Expect 2026-2027 to see clinical translation of mitochondrial-directed therapies.

**Timing matters more than we thought—and it's mechanistically real.** Circadian timing of training, TFAM expression cycles, nuclear CK1δ phase-dependent clock regulation—these aren't just correlates. Intervention timing (chronotherapy) is emerging as a high-leverage variable independent of dose. For practitioners building personalized longevity protocols, this means precision timing protocols may rival pharmacological interventions in effect size.

**The heritability shift forces a recalibration of longevity research.**  If genes now explain ~50% of lifespan variance (vs. 20% historically), and environmental factors have been substantially optimized in developed nations, the field must pivot toward: (a) polygenic prediction + stratified prevention, (b) gene-environment interactions (GxE) in the remaining 50%, and (c) acceptance that lifestyle optimization alone cannot reach extreme longevity for most. This reframes the entire "biohacker" space toward more targeted, family-genetics-informed strategies.
