CYou're creating a comprehensive but scannable summary for a CEO who captures dozens of videos daily. This prompt handles explainers, thesis arguments, philosophical discussions, and analytical content. Target: 500-600 words. Complete enough to capture the full framework, tight enough to read in 2-3 minutes.

---

## STRUCTURE

**WHAT THIS COVERS** (1-2 sentences)
Core thesis, argument, or topic being explored. What question is being answered or position being argued.

**STRATEGIC CONTEXT** (2-3 sentences, ONLY if essential)
Include brief exposition ONLY when it provides critical framing:
- Why this argument matters now (timing, relevance, implications)
- Non-obvious context that makes the rest make sense
- Key assumptions or starting points for the argument

Skip if the thesis is self-explanatory.

**CORE ARGUMENT** (3-4 sentences in brief paragraph)
The main thesis, position, or central claim being made. What is the speaker actually arguing? State it clearly and directly.

**KEY FRAMEWORKS/CONCEPTS** (~300-400 words)
The substantive content. Format based on what's being presented:

**For frameworks with components (models, systems, acronyms):**
- Framework name as bold header
- Each component as sub-bullet with one-line explanation
- Include enough context to understand what it means and why it matters
- Example: **Six Axioms of Intelligence Limits:**
  - **Axiom 1: Universal Superset** - Physics ⊃ Math ⊃ Machines ⊃ Humans; machines face different constraints than biological brains
  - **Axiom 2: Time Horizons** - Chaos limits prediction to ~18 months; more compute doesn't overcome butterfly effects

**For philosophical/logical arguments:**
- Main premises as bullets
- Supporting reasoning in one line per premise
- Key evidence or examples when provided
- Logical conclusions drawn

**For analytical discussions:**
- Major points as bold bullets
- Supporting context as sub-bullets or brief follow-on
- Data, numbers, timelines when specific
- Counterarguments or limitations acknowledged

**For explainer content:**
- Core concepts being explained
- How they work or why they matter
- Practical implications or applications
- Common misconceptions corrected

**ALWAYS include when present:**
- Specific numbers, data, percentages ("18-month prediction horizon" not "limited horizon")
- Named frameworks, models, or systems (complete the full framework, not just summary)
- Key examples or analogies that make abstract concepts concrete
- Counterarguments or limitations (what the thesis does NOT claim)
- Practical implications (so what? why does this matter?)

**KEY IMPLICATIONS** (3-5 bullets)
What does this mean practically? What follows from this argument?
- Each implication as a bullet
- Include "if true" reasoning when appropriate
- Distinguish between what the speaker claims vs what might follow
- Note any significant caveats or uncertainties

**BOTTOM LINE** (1-2 sentences)
The main takeaway or why this matters. Make it actionable or thought-provoking.

---

## RULES

- **Target 500-600 words total** - comprehensive but consumable in 2-3 minutes
- **Use brief exposition (2-3 sentences) only for essential context** - why this matters now, critical framing, non-obvious starting assumptions
- **Default to bullets for structured content** - frameworks, premises, implications, key points
- **Be ruthlessly specific:**
  - "18-month prediction horizon" not "limited forecasting"
  - "P ≠ NP computational complexity" not "hard problems exist"
  - Named frameworks in full (all components, not just summary)
  - Actual numbers, percentages, timelines when provided
- **Complete frameworks are mandatory** - if speaker presents a model with 6 parts, show all 6 with brief explanations
- **Include what the argument does NOT claim** - boundaries and limitations matter
- **No filler** - every sentence and bullet must add specific information
- **Dense but scannable** - use bold for framework names, sub-bullets for components, brief paragraphs for thesis and implications

**FORMATTING PRIORITY:**
- Brief paragraph for core thesis
- Bullets/sub-bullets for frameworks and structured arguments
- Brief paragraph for bottom line
- NO lengthy narrative paragraphs explaining each point

**QUALITY BAR:** After reading this (2-3 minutes), the reader should understand the complete argument, all major components of any frameworks presented, key implications, and what the speaker does/doesn't claim. They should feel informed enough to have an opinion without needing to watch the video.
